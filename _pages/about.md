My overarching research aims to design systems, control, and decision-making algorithms to develop safe, robust, and efficient dynamical systems. In particular, my research is based on control theory, optimization, and mathematical analysis, with a strong interest in understanding how rigorous theoretical tools can be translated into reliable real-world autonomous systems. I am especially interested in safety-critical control, robust and stochastic optimization, learning-based control, and the integration of perception and decision-making under uncertainty. I focus on bridging the gap between theory and practice by developing algorithms that are not only mathematically sound but also implementable and verifiable in real-time systems.

I am a vehicle motion control and autonomous driving engineer with more than ten years of experience in automotive and production-oriented engineering. At HL Mando, I lead and own the end-to-end development of planning and control functions—from algorithm architecture, modeling, and simulation to real-time implementation, vehicle integration, tuning, and validation.

My recent work includes Integrated Chassis Control, Vehicle Stability Control, Evasive Collision Avoidance, Smart Hitching Assist, Trailer Parking Assist, and Minimum Risk Maneuver. Across these projects, I have served as the technical lead and project owner, coordinating function definition, algorithm development, embedded implementation, system integration, and vehicle-level evaluation.

My technical background spans motion planning, path generation and tracking, vehicle dynamics and stability control, optimization-based control, and state estimation. I develop and analyze production software in C, use Python and MATLAB/Simulink for algorithm development, and deploy control models to dSPACE MicroAutoBox II real-time targets for in-vehicle testing. I also use NVIDIA Jetson platforms for vehicle evaluation and have experience with NVIDIA Isaac Sim.

I work within automotive software processes, including requirements-based development, unit and integration testing, model-based development, and vehicle-level verification against measurable performance criteria.

Alongside my industrial work, I am pursuing a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering at Seoul National University, where my research focuses on safe and robust planning and control under model and perception uncertainty.
